# Lamucca — Creative Direction

**Status:** adopted 2026-07-25 · the aesthetic law for the digital experience
**Upstream:** brand-strategy.md · product-strategy.md · information-architecture.md
**Method:** three radically different concepts, each grown from a different verified
brand truth. One winner, with organs transplanted from the losers.

---

# Part I — Three concepts

## CONCEPT 1 · «A DOS TINTAS»
*(“In two inks” — two-color printing as worldview)*

- **Rooted in (verified):** HIGH LOW philosophy; the brand's print-adjacent identity
  (American Typewriter, dictionary entries, "Muckero del mes" as magazine franchise);
  the founder duality (theatre + engineering); editorial voice with invented words.
- **1. Name:** A DOS TINTAS.
- **2. Core idea:** The Lamucca digital experience is a living piece of print — a
  neighborhood publication pressed in exactly two inks: **ink** (the HIGH: editorial
  serif, craft, restraint) and **tomato** (the LOW: street, appetite, buen rollo).
  Every screen is a page; every visit is today's edition. The philosophy isn't
  explained — it's *printed into everything*.
- **3. Emotional promise:** "I'm holding something made with care by people who don't
  take themselves seriously." Warmth + wit + trust — the feeling of a beloved city
  magazine that knows your name.
- **4. Visual language:** warm paper, warm-black ink, tomato as the second ink; duotone
  (two-ink) photography treatment unifying all imagery; hard 1.5px rules and borders;
  registration marks, stamps, dictionary entries, tickers as press artifacts; Fraunces
  (HIGH voice) ↔ Archivo caps (LOW voice) in deliberate tension on every page.
- **5. Storytelling approach:** editorial formats as narrative devices — the dictionary
  entry (muckero), the front-page headline (hero), the timeline strip (2008→now), the
  classified ad (careers: "SE BUSCA: sonrisa imborrable"), the interview pull-quote
  (Ofelia). Chapters read like sections of a paper, not sections of a website.
- **6. Motion philosophy:** *the press, not the cursor*. Elements stamp, settle, slide
  like paper on a press bed; the ticker rolls like a print ribbon; reveals are ink
  drying (fast fade-settle, 200–700ms). Nothing floats, nothing blurs, nothing loops
  except tickers. Reduced motion = the printed page, already dry.
- **7. Navigation philosophy:** the adopted "editorial spine, contextual booking" —
  five doors as a masthead; Reservar as the red stamp always on the desk.
- **8. Hero concept:** a front page. Masthead line (GRUPO DE RESTAURANTES · MADRID ·
  DESDE 2008), the headline set enormous ("High en producto, *low* en pretensión."),
  edition line that changes with Madrid's clock ("Edición del aperitivo — 13:07,
  Madrid"), one red stamp: RESERVAR.
- **9. Restaurant discovery concept:** "las portadas" — each casa is a magazine cover
  in the family style: accent ink, year, barrio, tagline, two traits. Same paper,
  different covers — identical structure never identical character. The picker
  (¿Cuál te toca?) is the paper's horoscope: answer the question, get your casa.
- **10. Booking concept:** the red stamp. A tomato-ink stamp mark ("RESERVAR") that
  travels the whole experience (nav, cards, sticky bar, sheet). Stamping = booking;
  the confirmation feeling of ink hitting paper. The booking sheet is a printed order
  slip: casa covers, not a dropdown.
- **11. Muckero concept:** the community *is the staff box* of the publication —
  muckeros as bylined contributors. Dictionary entry as founding text; Muckero del mes
  as recurring column with portrait + nickname; counters as circulation figures
  ("1.204.331 pizzas de boletus servidas"); "Hazte muckero" = subscribe to the paper.

## CONCEPT 2 · «SIEMPRE ES LA HORA»
*(“It's always the hour” — the site as Madrid's living clock)*

- **Rooted in (verified):** "cocina abierta todo el día"; the brand's own verb chain
  ("¿Desayunar, Aperitivear, Comer, Tapear, Merendar, Cenar y Recenar?"); music in the
  muckero definition; day-to-night venues ("cenas que acaban en baile"; Club
  Fishermans; Makáá).
- **1. Name:** SIEMPRE ES LA HORA.
- **2. Core idea:** the experience is synchronized to Madrid's actual clock. The site
  you see at 09:00 (café con leche light, En Bruto bread, coworking Fuencarral) is not
  the site you see at 13:30 (vermut, terraza, aperitivo) or at 23:40 (ink dark, neon
  coral, "cenas que acaban en baile", Club Fishermans surfaces). Content, accents,
  recommendations and even the verb in the hero ("Ahora toca: aperitivear") rotate
  through seven day-parts. The world is literally alive.
- **3. Emotional promise:** "This place is awake when I am." Aliveness, appetite-timing,
  the thrill of a city that never fully closes.
- **4. Visual language:** one typographic system, seven light temperatures — paper
  warms and darkens with the sun; accents rotate (butter mornings, tomato aperitivo,
  olive afternoons, ink-and-coral nights); photography keyed by daypart.
- **5. Storytelling approach:** the day as narrative spine — the homepage is 24 hours
  in the life of the family, told hour by hour; venue pages state what each casa is
  *for* at each hour.
- **6. Motion philosophy:** slow ambient shifts (light changing across a room) + sharp
  hour-tick moments; a persistent subtle clock/metronome motif; music-adjacent rhythm
  in staggered reveals.
- **7. Navigation philosophy:** contextual-first — "Ahora" is the implicit first door
  (what's right *now*), with the five stable doors behind it.
- **8. Hero concept:** the live line: "Madrid, 13:07. **Ahora toca aperitivear.**" +
  the casa that owns this hour + Reservar. The hero is never twice the same.
- **9. Restaurant discovery concept:** casas plotted on the day — a horizontal
  day-strip (07:00–03:00); drag the hour, casas light up when they're at their best.
- **10. Booking concept:** time-first: "¿Para ahora, para luego, para otro día?" —
  then casa. Booking module pre-seeded with the chosen slot.
- **11. Muckero concept:** muckeros of the hour — the barista, the vermutero, the DJ;
  community rendered as the people who run each hour of Madrid.
- **Honest risks:** first-time comprehension (the site never looks the same twice —
  screenshots, shares and support get harder); content ops burden (7 dayparts × 18
  casas); daypart logic can feel gimmicky abroad/mismatched timezones; harder to keep
  CWV pristine with contextual swaps. A brilliant *feature*, a risky *foundation*.

## CONCEPT 3 · «LOS LOCALES LLAMAN»
*(“The spaces call” — eighteen doors with past lives)*

- **Rooted in (verified):** the real growth logic ("Nunca hemos tenido una estrategia…
  los locales nos llaman"); every venue's previous life (a dead tahona, a Bourguignon
  flower shop, a derelict fishmonger's, a 19th-century café, a 1907 kiosk, a hotel
  rooftop); Ofelia's theatre origin; interiors that preserve memory (graffiti next to
  art déco).
- **1. Name:** LOS LOCALES LLAMAN.
- **2. Core idea:** the experience is un paseo — a walk through Madrid pushing doors.
  Each casa is a door; behind each door, a space that had a previous life and kept its
  ghost. The site is staged like theatre (Ofelia's craft): each venue page is a stage
  set that "remembers" being a bakery, a florist, a fishmonger. You don't browse a
  website; you walk streets and doors open.
- **3. Emotional promise:** "Madrid is full of secret rooms and I have the key."
  Discovery, intimacy, the shiver of a city with layers.
- **4. Visual language:** cinematic photography-first (rooms in low warm light);
  door/threshold as the recurring frame; deep space and shadow; typography as street
  signage and old shop lettering; each casa's past life expressed in texture.
- **5. Storytelling approach:** every page is a scene with a before/after — "esto era
  una tahona" staged as reveal; founders as the two characters who keep opening doors
  they shouldn't.
- **6. Motion philosophy:** thresholds — doors, curtains, lights coming up on a set;
  slow dolly-like image reveals; scroll as walking pace.
- **7. Navigation philosophy:** spatial — the street/map is the primary browse mode;
  the five doors exist but the map is home's heart.
- **8. Hero concept:** a threshold: the Pez doorway at dusk, sound of the room implied;
  "Empuja la puerta." as the only instruction.
- **9. Restaurant discovery concept:** the walk — an illustrated/photographic Madrid
  street-map; hover/tap a door and the room glows with its tagline and past life.
- **10. Booking concept:** "Te abrimos la puerta" — booking as being let in;
  confirmation framed as a key.
- **11. Muckero concept:** the people behind the doors — portraits at thresholds
  (waiters mid-service, the baker at the obrador); community as the city's doormen who
  know your name.
- **Honest risks:** it *is* the immersive temptation the four fives warn about — its
  quality depends almost entirely on commissioned photography/film we don't yet have;
  map-first navigation taxes Journeys 3/6 (speed) and mobile; hardest to keep fast;
  closest to "luxury cinematic" drift if executed 5% wrong.

# Part II — Selection

## Winner: «A DOS TINTAS»

**Why it is the most authentic.** It is built from the brand's own artifacts, not a
theme applied to them: they already write dictionary entries, run a magazine franchise
(Muckero del mes), set their identity in a typewriter face, publish circulation-style
counters, and define themselves by a two-register philosophy. A DOS TINTAS doesn't
illustrate HIGH LOW — it *is* HIGH LOW, structurally: two inks, two type voices, two
registers on every page. Even the founders map onto it (theatre = the front page,
engineering = the press).

**Why it is commercially effective.** Editorial systems are conversion-native: clear
hierarchy, scannable pages, text-first speed, and a persistent red stamp (Reservar)
that behaves like the best CTA pattern there is. It serves all six journeys without
tension — direct bookers get front-loaded action; discoverers get a paper worth
reading. And it's operationally cheap per venue: a new casa is a new cover in the
system, not a new set to build.

**Why it is memorable.** Nobody in the category looks like this. Madrid competitors
are Elementor brochures or offline; Big Mamma owns maximalist Italian collage. A
restaurant group whose website is a living two-ink neighborhood paper — with editions,
stamps, horoscope-picker and classifieds — is instantly describable in one sentence
("their site is like a newspaper you can book tables from"), which is the practical
definition of memorable.

**Why it is technically achievable.** It is typography, two colors, borders and a
duotone image treatment — all native to CSS and static rendering. No WebGL, no
scroll-jacking, no runtime cost. The duotone treatment even *solves* a real problem:
it makes their uneven existing photography (WhatsApp-named JPEGs) look intentional and
unified until new photography is commissioned. The current 0-JS-bundle build already
speaks this language; nothing gets thrown away.

**Why it could become a signature digital experience.** It has an ownable mechanic
(the edition that changes with Madrid's clock), an ownable gesture (the stamp as
booking), an ownable voice-device (dictionary entries, classifieds), and an ownable
image treatment (two-ink duotone). Those four are a brand system others can't lift
without becoming Lamucca. It can grow for years — new "sections" of the paper (craft,
journal, membership) without redesign — and it degrades gracefully: even its plainest
page still looks like the concept.

**Transplants from the losing concepts** (their best organs, adopted):
- From SIEMPRE ES LA HORA → **the edition mechanic**: the paper's masthead line and
  accent temperature shift across dayparts ("Edición de la mañana / del aperitivo /
  de la noche"), and the picker gains an "ahora toca" awareness. The living-clock idea
  survives as a *device*, without betting the foundation on it.
- From LOS LOCALES LLAMAN → **the past-life scene**: every venue page keeps a staged
  "esto era…" moment (tahona/floristería/kiosco) as its story layer, and the future
  commissioned photography brief is thresholds-and-rooms, duotone-ready.

# Part III — Creative Direction Document: «A DOS TINTAS»

## 1. The law

**Every page is a printed page. Every printed page carries both inks.**
Ink (HIGH): Fraunces, editorial restraint, craft. Tomato (LOW): Archivo caps, stamps,
street energy. A screen with only one ink is off-concept; a screen where the inks
don't *talk* to each other is off-concept.

## 2. Palette (evolves tokens.css, doesn't replace it)

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | #F4EEE3 | the page |
| `--paper-deep` | #ECE3D2 | cards, alt sections |
| `--ink` | #201B16 | first ink: text, rules, borders |
| `--tomato` | #D2401E | second ink: stamps, accents, CTAs |
| `--tomato-deep` | #B23317 | small-text-safe tomato |
| `--coral` | #FF6863 | the heart only (brand mark, ticker separators) |
| `--butter` / `--olive` | #E8B940 / #5D6141 | casa accents + edition temperature |

Edition temperature (the transplanted clock): mañana = butter-warmed paper accents,
aperitivo/tarde = tomato, noche = ink sections deepen, coral glows. Implemented as a
`data-edition` attribute swapping accent variables — cosmetic, cacheable, never
layout-shifting, and fully ignorable by first-time users.

## 3. Typography

- **Fraunces Variable** = the HIGH ink voice: headlines, quotes, venue names,
  the `voice` italic. Big sizes lean into optical warmth (SOFT/WONK welcome at 56px+,
  never in body).
- **Archivo Variable** = the LOW ink voice: labels, wayfinding, stamps, tickers,
  UI, body text. Caps + 0.10–0.14em tracking for signage; never for paragraphs.
- The two voices must co-occur: every composed block pairs them (headline + kicker,
  quote + byline, cover + stamp).
- Scale: existing fluid steps (--step--1 … --step-6). Body ≥16px mobile always.

## 4. Press artifacts (the component vocabulary)

The reusable set — build once, use everywhere:
1. **La cabecera** (masthead): kicker line with rules + edition line with Madrid time.
2. **El sello** (the stamp): Reservar as tomato stamp — slight rotation (-2°),
   stamp-settle animation on entrance, hover = re-ink (darken). The one red object.
3. **La portada** (cover card): venue card as magazine cover — accent field, year,
   barrio, tagline, traits; 1.5px ink border; print-block hover shadow (existing).
4. **La entrada de diccionario**: definition card (muckero device; reusable for
   HIGH LOW, "aperitivear", future words).
5. **El teletipo** (ticker): marquee with ♥ separators; chapter divider duty.
6. **El anuncio clasificado**: bordered mono-spaced-feel box for careers/eventos
   one-liners ("SE BUSCA: sonrisa imborrable. Razón: aquí.").
7. **La cita** (pull quote): Fraunces italic monument with label byline.
8. **La tira** (timeline strip): 2008→now horizontal scrub, one beat per casa.
9. **Marcas de registro**: corner registration marks / crop marks as section
   punctuation (decorative, aria-hidden, sparing — one set per chapter max).

## 5. Image treatment

- **Duotone law:** all photography renders in two-ink duotone by default (ink +
  paper, or casa-accent + paper) via CSS filter/blend — full color is *earned*, used
  only at the moment of appetite (venue-page food/room gallery on interaction, and
  future commissioned heroes).
- This unifies legacy photography instantly and makes future art direction cheap.
- Alt text = descriptive sentences, never filenames (fixes audit finding).
- Commissioned-photo brief (future): thresholds, rooms in service, hands, faces;
  never sterile food-on-white.

## 6. Motion philosophy (extends decision 008)

The press, not the cursor:
- **Stamp-settle** (scale 1.06→1 + tiny rotation to rest, 250ms ease-snap): stamps,
  dictionary card, badges.
- **Ink-dry reveal** (existing translateY+fade, 500–700ms ease-out): editorial blocks.
- **Press-slide** (x-axis enter for strips/tickers): la tira, el teletipo.
- **Re-ink** (color deepen 150–200ms): all hovers. No scale-on-hover layout shifts.
- Budget: one narrative motion per chapter; micro ≤300ms; narrative ≤800ms;
  loops = tickers only; `prefers-reduced-motion` = the page already printed (all
  content visible, tickers static).

## 7. Voice in the interface

- Microcopy carries the paper conceit sparingly: editions, "última hora" for
  openings, "suscríbete" → "Hazte muckero", 404 = "Esta página se nos quemó en el
  horno" + concierge entry.
- Verbatim texts remain sacred (dictionary, HIGH LOW, quotes).
- Never more than one joke per screen; Reservar copy is never joked with.

## 8. The seven chapters, in this language

01 La Puerta = front page (masthead, headline, edition line, stamp) ·
02 Las Casas = the covers ·
03 La Pregunta = the horoscope (chips as stamps, inline preview) ·
04 La Tahona = the interview (ink section, la cita, la tira) ·
05 El Manifiesto = the two-ink spread (pure type, inks in opposition) ·
06 Los Muckeros = the staff box (dictionary, bylines, circulation counters) ·
07 El Universo = the index/classifieds (list rows + clasificado for eventos).

## 9. Do / Never

**Do:** hard borders, real facts as content, two inks in tension, stamps for actions,
duotone imagery, edition line with real Madrid time, casa accents inside the system.
**Never:** gradients-as-decoration, glassmorphism/blur, gold/luxury coding, uniform
venue tiles, full-color stock food photography, scroll-jacking, more than one red
object competing with Reservar, paraphrased brand quotes, fake counters.

## 10. Definition of "on-concept" (acceptance test)

A screen passes if: (1) you could imagine it printed and pinned in a Malasaña kitchen;
(2) both inks are present and in tension; (3) the red stamp (or its absence) is
deliberate; (4) it loads like text, not like an app; (5) a muckero would smirk at
something on it.
